# Milestone v1.2 — SDLC Coverage & Foundation Guards: Lifecycle-Coverage Audit

**Audited:** 2026-06-09
**Status:** `roadmap-sufficient` — every real lifecycle gap maps to a v1.2 phase (11–17); zero gaps are uncovered. This audit confirms the roadmap; it does **not** re-scope or rewrite it (D-03).

This is a clear-voice engineering artifact — an internal `.planning/` self-assessment of grugops's own kit (D-01). It is not shipped in the kit and does not live under `docs/`. The integrity contract here is the no-fabrication rule (CLAUDE.md): every coverage cell below was scored from the actually-read role/workflow content, never invented; any cell that could not be verified reads `UNKNOWN - verify`.

## Scope & Method

- **Subjects:** all 16 roles under `agent-factory/roles/` (excluding `_role-switch-protocol.md`, which is shared activation plumbing, not a lifecycle actor) and all 14 workflows under `agent-factory/workflows/`.
- **Columns:** the 9 canonical lifecycle stages named verbatim in `PROJECT.md` — business analysis → product → system analysis → architecture → engineering → QE/E2E → security/NFR/compliance → UAT → release (D-02).
- **Method:** each role was scored from its `## One job`, `## Activates when`, `## Responsibilities`, and `## Board moves` sections; each workflow from its `## When to use`, flow line, and `## Agents involved`. Scoring is conservative: `●` is reserved for the primary owner of a stage, `◐` for a stage that is touched but shallow / advisory / trigger-gated, and `—` for an honest non-owner (out of scope for that role — **not** automatically a lifecycle hole).
- **Distinguishing a `—` from a real gap:** a column of all-`—` for a single role is normal (single-job roles). A *lifecycle* gap is a stage that is **shallow across every owner** (e.g. an acceptance contract that exists only as prose) or a capability the milestone goal calls for that no role/workflow currently owns (e.g. a UI specialist). Only those become rows in the "Gaps → addressing phase" table.

**Legend:** `●` covered (primary owner) · `◐` partial (touched, shallow/advisory/triggered) · `—` gap (out of scope for this row).

## Per-Gap Narrative

The audit finds four real lifecycle gaps. Each is a depth/contract/specialization hole within an otherwise-owned stage — not a missing owner.

### GAP-1 — business→engineer handoff is prose-only (named focus of the milestone)

**Where:** between stage 2 (product, owned by `ba-pm`) and stage 5 (engineering, owned by `software-engineer`), carried through `03-epic-to-tickets` and `06-uat-pack`.

**What's there today:** `03-epic-to-tickets` already produces "Given/When/Then acceptance criteria" on each ticket, and `ba-pm` takes each ticket to a Definition of Ready. So the *intent* of a business→engineer contract exists.

**The hole:** the acceptance criteria are **prose only** — there is no executable acceptance contract and no Three Amigos / Example Mapping step that produces the scenarios *before* code, and no rule binding a unit-test layer to those scenarios. The engineer reads the ready-packet, but nothing makes "the behavior the business asked for" mechanically verifiable. This is exactly the milestone's named focus: the business→engineer handoff is the weakest seam in the lifecycle.

**Why it matters:** a prose-only acceptance criterion is the classic place where business intent and engineering output drift. Without an executable-or-absent contract and a test-layer ownership rule, the handoff is trust, not proof.

### GAP-2 — persona depth: no explicit senior-judgment layer; BA persona shallow

**Where:** cross-cutting across all 16 roles; most acute at stage 1 (BA) and stage 2 (product).

**What's there today:** every role has a tight `## One job`, a caveman prompt, and real hard limits — the roles are *correct* and single-purpose.

**The hole:** no role carries an explicit, clear-voice "what good looks like / when to escalate" judgment section, so the personas read as competent-junior rather than senior. The business-analysis persona in particular is shallow relative to the milestone's senior-grade goal — no INVEST-shaped story discipline, no measurable-NFR habit baked into the persona itself.

**Why it matters:** the milestone goal is "senior-grade and trustworthy end-to-end." Senior judgment (knowing when *not* to act, when to escalate) is the difference between a role that executes and a role you'd trust unsupervised.

### GAP-3 — no frontend/UI specialist and no UI design→build flow

**Where:** stage 5 (engineering). The single `software-engineer` role owns all of engineering; there is no 17th UI role and no UI-specific workflow.

**What's there today:** `software-engineer` (`●` at stage 5) implements any ticket, UI or not, and `qe-e2e` mentions stable selectors / E2E.

**The hole:** there is no senior frontend/UI persona and no design-contract → component-build → states (loading/empty/error/success/partial) → accessibility → visual-baseline workflow. UI work is implicitly folded into the generalist engineer with no specialist discipline.

**Why it matters:** the milestone explicitly adds a UI build flow. UI has its own failure modes (states, a11y, visual regression) that a generalist engineering persona does not systematically cover.

### GAP-4 — security/NFR is review-only (no leveled evidence anchor) and the gate's QE depth is thin

**Where:** stage 7 (security/NFR/compliance, owned by `security-nfr` + `compliance-officer`) and stage 6 (QE/E2E) as exercised through `05-pr-quality-gate`.

**What's there today:** `security-nfr` (`●` at stage 7) returns `PASS | PASS_WITH_RISKS | BLOCKED`; `compliance-officer` classifies data and maps PII; `05-pr-quality-gate` is the single-source backpressure loop. The stage has owners.

**The hole:** the security review is **judgment-only** — it is not anchored to a leveled, evidence-backed standard (OWASP ASVS), so a "pass" is not tied to a requirement ID or a level. And the §14 gate's quality depth is thin: no first-class lint step, no automated UI/E2E + visual regression, and no un-cheatable test-integrity check (unjustified skipped tests can slip through). The dial keys to control all of this (`security.asvs_level`, `security.block_on`, `quality.tdd`, `quality.lint`, `quality.ui_e2e`, `quality.test_integrity`) do not yet exist.

**Why it matters:** "trustworthy end-to-end" requires that a security pass cite evidence and that the gate cannot be cheated. Review-by-vibes and a skippable gate are the two ways quality discipline silently erodes.

## The business→engineer handoff (named focus)

The milestone opens with this audit specifically to confirm the lifecycle's weakest seam is closed. That seam is **GAP-1**: the business→engineer handoff is real (acceptance criteria + Definition of Ready exist) but **prose-only** — there is no executable acceptance contract and nothing binds a unit-test layer to the business's stated behavior.

**Closed by Phase 12 (BDD + TDD Wiring):** given-when-then acceptance scenarios produced by a Three Amigos / Example Mapping step *before* code (the business→engineer contract), carried in the product and QE handoff templates and wired executable-or-absent, driving a red-green TDD double-loop at the unit layer — each behavior owned by exactly one test layer, both depths config-dialed (`bdd`, `quality.tdd`). The roadmap places Phase 12 directly after the senior-persona substrate (Phase 11) and after this phase's dial keys (Phase 10), which is the correct dependency order: the contract needs senior personas to author it and dial keys to gate it.

## Verdict

**`roadmap-sufficient`.** The kit's lifecycle **breadth** is complete: every one of the 9 canonical stages has at least one primary owner among the 16 roles + 14 workflows. The four real gaps the audit finds are **depth / contract / specialization** holes — a prose-only business→engineer contract (GAP-1, the named focus), a missing senior-judgment persona layer (GAP-2), no frontend/UI specialist (GAP-3), and a review-only security posture with a thin quality gate (GAP-4). Each maps cleanly to a phase already in the v1.2 roadmap (12, 11, 13, 14+15 respectively). **Zero gaps are uncovered by the roadmap**, so no re-scope is warranted — this audit confirms the roadmap as planned and explicitly does not rewrite it (D-03).

Two doc defects are noted as observations only (their fixes are owned elsewhere, not re-scoped here): the stale "Orchestrator spawns role agents" prose in `adapters.md` (fixed in Plan 10-02 per D-09) and the WR-05 packaging-template spawn-grant regeneration hazard (guarded in Phase 10, retired in Phase 11).
