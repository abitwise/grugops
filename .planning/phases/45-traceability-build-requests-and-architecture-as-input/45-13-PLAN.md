<objective>
Rewrite Workflow 20's floor rule so it holds with no side door and one floor list (gaps G-45-1 and G-45-9).

G-45-1: the floor stop fires before a build request can exist. The rule sentence and the stop bullet never put the BA/PM hand-off (`## Build requests` steps 1-5) before the stop, so a literal reader withholds the BA/PM subtask; the live probe filed no request and the Orchestrator wrote the `## Blocked` bullet itself. The debug session's differential run showed that rewording just those two places makes the Orchestrator dispatch BA/PM, file BR-1 with the contract bullet, and then stop.

G-45-9: once filed, a floor-conflict request is an ordinary request. BA/PM may withdraw it alone (step 13), step 12 then unblocks the ticket, and every program goes green and silent. The floor list was transcribed from research rather than pointed at the canonical floors, and the given-input sentences state the rule with no floor exception.

Human routing at UAT (2026-11-26, binding): decision 1, a named human's withdrawal of an `open` request is a recorded form (plan 45-12 changes the grammar), and Workflow 20 says BA/PM alone never withdraws a deviation or floor-conflict request, by extending the existing human's-word stop bullet so WORKFLOW_STOP_BULLET_COUNT stays 58. Decision 2, Workflow 20 points at the canonical floors and also names the quality, security and NFR gates, and the undefined write-verification term goes. Decision 3, text fix only: no new program check (the 45-10 decision and accepted risk T-45-35 stay). Review finding WR-03 (step 12 ignores a ticket's other blockers) is adopted here because the human-stop bullet depends on it.

How a deviation or floor-conflict request is told apart, and why BA/PM alone cannot unblock one (planner's mechanism under decision 1; plan-check revision 1). A request file has no field for it (D-05 closed key set). Two marks are used:
- The guard that holds the property is the human-stop bullet. The role that records the deviation or floor-conflict observation writes it on each affected ticket: the Architect for a deviation (D-16), the role that finds a floor conflict otherwise. BA/PM, the only role that withdraws, never writes it. It is owned by a named human and names no request, so a withdrawal and step 12 never remove it. With WR-03, step 12 moves a ticket to `Backlog` only when no other bullet remains. A BA/PM-alone withdrawal, even one written in error against step 13, therefore leaves the ticket `Blocked`. Only a named human's word removes the human-stop bullet, and the human's-word stop bullet refuses an agent that asks otherwise.
- The second mark is BA/PM's own wording. Step 4 makes BA/PM say in `## Question` that the request records a deviation or a floor conflict. Step 13 and the stop bullet reserve the withdrawal of such a request to a named human, so the request's History names that human.

The one residual is a writing error by the observing role, which fails to write the human-stop bullet at all. No program checks for it, because human decision 3 rules out a new check. It is accepted as T-45-47, next to T-45-35. It is not a BA/PM-alone path, and the Done condition names the bullet.

The board contract's build-request bullet keeps `(owner: human carrier)`: the carry, the answer and every deviation or floor-conflict withdrawal need that human, and BA/PM's lone withdrawal is limited to a duplicate or unneeded question, as D-06 chose.

Human decision 2 replaces the parenthetical floor list that D-17, REQUIREMENTS ARCH-02 and ROADMAP Phase 45 success criterion 5 carry. Record that in the SUMMARY under key-decisions; amending those planning texts is left to the human.

Purpose: a deviation or floor-conflict ticket is held by a request that exists and by a stop only a named human lifts, and no upstream decision moves a floor.
Output: the Workflow 20 rewrite, the human-stop bullet form in the board contract, the text tests, the disposition rows, and WR-03 marked fixed.
