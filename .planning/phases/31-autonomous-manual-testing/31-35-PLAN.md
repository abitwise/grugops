<objective>
Make a walk that hit its own bound say so, and route the outcome to could-not-run rather than to a
pass.

CR-25 (UATX-06, UATX-05). `frameworkSurface` bounds its breadth-first walk at
`SURFACE_DEPTH_BOUND = 6` and `SURFACE_NODE_BOUND = 4096` and returns a partial surface with no
signal. A framework member the walk never reached is absent from `ctx.frameworkFiles`, so
`resolveBannedModifier` classifies every call on it `foreign` — accept. The comment claims the bounds
mean a large surface `costs a stated amount rather than an open one`; the cost is not an amount, it is
the ban turning off. Both `31-REVIEW.md` and `31-VERIFICATION.md` round 7 reproduced and bisected it
independently: one chain length refused at exit 1, the next accepted at exit 0 with zero bytes on
stderr.

This is the identical silent degrade D-30 sub-decision 2 was written to eliminate for RR-07,
reintroduced one register over inside the mechanism that eliminated it — and unlike RR-07 it is
disclosed nowhere: neither the recipe's boundary list nor `UNRESOLVABLE_CALLEE_RESIDUALS` mentions
either bound.

The two independent bisections disagree about the numeric boundary (6/7 versus 7/8) because they
declare the surface across files differently, and the verifier records a second population path for
`frameworkFiles` that a naive construction never escapes. Reconciling them to one number, with the
construction written down, is part of the work rather than an afterthought.

Purpose: an absent or unusable analysis must produce a loud skip that leaves the UAT pending, never a
silent pass — which is UATX-05 stated for the analyser rather than for the browser.
Output: a `truncated` signal routed to exit 2 with its own cause; both bounds as named register
members and published boundaries; a two-sided corpus row; one reconciled boundary number; decision
`D-36` recorded.
</objective>
