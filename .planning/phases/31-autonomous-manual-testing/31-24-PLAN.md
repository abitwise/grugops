<objective>
Close **CR-14**, **WR-23**, **WR-24** and **IN-12** of `31-REVIEW.md` — one scoping rule and its corpus,
plus one duplicate-normalisation cleanup in the same function family.

Round 4's `31-17` closed WR-20 — a legitimate spec refused because the canonicaliser rewrote a head
segment with no scope analysis at all — by adding `deriveDeclaredNames`, a per-file census of the names
a spec declares, consulted before either map. That closure is real. What it cost is CR-14: the census is
FILE-scoped, so one declaration anywhere in a file suppresses the rewrite everywhere in it, and for a
BAN a suppressed rewrite is an ADMITTED construct. The verifier measured the consequence: a dead
`const it = 1;` inside a callback body turns `it.skip(...)` at module scope from `1 finding(s)`/exit 1
into `0 findings`/exit 0. WR-14 and CR-10 were each Critical; both are now two lines away from where
they were before `31-13` and `31-16`.

The cost is disclosed — the residual register states it, the recipe repeats it, and the suite asserts it
green under the heading "THE COARSENESS, MEASURED rather than described". That is why this is a blocker
rather than a residual: the disposition is UNPRICED. The register says a declaration suppresses the
rewrite for the whole file; it does not say that this is a two-line, deliberately-writable evasion of the
entire rename/namespace/fixture-parameter family. And the header sentence a future reader will rely on —
"THE RULE IS MONOTONE IN THE SAFE DIRECTION" — is inverted for a ban.

WR-23 is the same rule one position over: the second-parameter exemption is stated for ANY function-like
node rather than for the callback the fixture map reads, so a helper with a second parameter sharing a
renamed name reopens WR-20 verbatim — a legitimate spec refused, the finding naming a construct absent
from the file. It is closed in the SAME edit because the two are two faces of one scoping rule.

WR-24 is why neither was caught: the new fixture's contract is "stay at zero findings", so a fix that
disabled the canonicalisation entirely keeps it green. The union — one file that both declares the name
and genuinely calls the modifier through the rename — is asserted nowhere in the corpus.

Purpose: make the suppression exactly as narrow as the false positive it exists for, and make the corpus
able to fail in both directions.

Output: a containment-based scope authority consumed by both canonicalisations; a narrowed
fixture-binding position; a fixture that can fail for CR-14's reason; one normalisation computed once;
a corrected header direction claim, residual sentence and recipe boundary list; and decision **D-27**
appended to `31-CONTEXT.md`.
