This is the FOURTH recurrence inside Phase 31 of a single pattern: a predicate is closed at the exact
coordinates a verifier measured, and reappears one register over. D-17 fixed MEMBERSHIP. D-18 fixed
SHAPE RESOLUTION. The register that failed this round is **which arms the resolved shape is compared
against**: D-18 (1) inserts a `()` marker segment into the resolved path and reasoned about the
consequence for exactly one of the ban's three arms. The other two arms compare a whole literal path,
and the marker adds a segment to that string — so `expect.configure({ retries: 2 }).soft(locator)`
resolves to `expect.configure().soft`, which is not a member of `BANNED_EXACT_PATHS`, and
`expect.configure({ retries: 2 }).configure({ soft: true })(locator)` resolves to
`expect.configure().configure`, which is not a key of `BANNED_CONFIGURED_PATHS`. Both are real soft
assertions: the lane goes green because the acceptance criterion failed. That is precisely what
D-14 arm (c) and D-17's `expect.soft` member exist to refuse.

The second axis is a MEMBERSHIP miss on a path that resolves perfectly. `testInfo.skip()` through the
second callback parameter is Playwright's primary documented spelling of the very modifiers D-18 (1)
was convened to decide in their `test.info()` form. It resolves cleanly to `testInfo.skip`; the head
is simply not a banned head. It was considered and dispositioned — but only inside a test-file
comment, hiding behind the exported alias residual whose stated reason ("cannot be followed to its
declaration without a type checker") is the exact excuse D-18 (3) disproved one shape earlier for
`ImportSpecifier.propertyName`. The second parameter of a function passed as the second argument to a
`test(...)` call is TestInfo BY POSITION, and the position is a literal in the source text.

ways to reach it are. Closing the three spellings without closing the two AXES would be the fifth
round of the same edit.
