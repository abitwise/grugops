ROOT CAUSE (confirmed in source, scripts/context-io.ts at HEAD e57e870): `isBoundaryAt(i)` (lines 416-420)
hard-requires `looksLikeFrontmatterLine(lines[i + 1])` — the line IMMEDIATELY after the `---`. But

line; and `opensIdBearingRun` (406-415) skips a leading blank but bails on junk. So splitNotes' boundary
trigger is a STRICT SUBSET of parseNote's fence grammar — narrower than the parser, for the 7th time.

THE META-DISEASE this round ends (the whole point): splitNotes RE-DERIVES "where does a note open" with
bespoke line heuristics (`isBoundaryShapedLine` + `looksLikeFrontmatterLine(lines[i+1])` + a hand-rolled

appeared. A green vitest suite has NOT been proof for this invariant, six times running.

there is ONE fence grammar; splitNotes' boundary-finding and parseNote CANNOT drift because the boundary
decision is expressed via parseNote's ACTUAL grammar, not a parallel line heuristic.

1. RIP OUT the bespoke boundary authority — the `looksLikeFrontmatterLine(lines[i + 1])` gate and the
   hand-rolled `opensIdBearingRun` line scan must NO LONGER be the thing that decides "does a note open
   here." (Helper predicates may survive as parseNote internals, but they must not be the boundary oracle.)

   parseNote's regex `^---\n([\s\S]*?)\n---` picks — the FIRST `\n---` after the open), hand it to
   parseNote, and treat it as a note boundary IFF parseNote(region) is non-null AND the parsed
   frontmatter carries an `id` (id-bearing). One grammar, consulted directly — no re-derivation to drift.
