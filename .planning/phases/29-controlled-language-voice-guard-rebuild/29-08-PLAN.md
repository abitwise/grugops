<objective>
Apply the writing profile to the first six workflows — the bootstrap pair, the two planning workflows,
the ticket-to-PR workflow, and the quality gate — turning the largest concentration of non-conforming
step bullets in the kit into the canonical form.

D-18 chose full-corpus scope deliberately, with this cost visible: a narrower initial scan set widened
later is how scan sets go stale. D-36 then bounded what "full corpus" means, and D-42 removed the one
generated file from it, so this plan works against a real 47-file denominator rather than an inflated
one.

The shape of the work is set by what the corpus actually looks like. There is no head to the first-word
distribution to adopt — 222 distinct first words over 421 bullets, 176 of them appearing exactly once,
and the nine most frequent are all non-verbs — so the profile declares the canonical step form and this
plan conforms text to it. The single largest non-conforming group is the bold-label grammar at 41 of
125 bullets, concentrated in the quality-gate workflow in this batch, and it is also the cheapest to
convert: the label comes off and the verb moves to position zero.

Two boundaries matter more than the volume. The check is section-scoped, so the noun-phrase bullets
under `## Inputs required` and the conditional bullets under `## Stop conditions` are correct as they
stand and must not be reshaped into imperatives — rewriting them would be conforming to a rule the
profile does not make. And every workflow's `## Stop conditions` and `## Commit` sections are frozen-set
sources, so a changed clause in either is refused unless its companion edit lands in the same commit.
D-02 is why they are touched at all rather than frozen: safety sections receive the profile
deliberately, one judgement at a time, because leaving the kit's most load-bearing prose as its least
controlled text inverts the requirement at the point where two agents most need to reach the same act.

Purpose: convert the first third of the governed corpus and prove the conversion is measurable per file.
Output: six conforming workflows, a per-file growth record, and a disposition file.
</objective>

Work one file at a time, and record each file's byte count before and after. The quality-gate workflow
is the largest file in the corpus at 13,831 bytes and carries the bold-label concentration, so take it
last, when the conversion pattern from the five smaller files is established.

For each file, in this order.

First, the `## Steps` bullets. Strip any leading bold label and promote the verb to position zero; where
the bullet opens with a determiner or an actor subject, restate it as an instruction to the reader
rather than a description of who does what — the actor is already established by the workflow's
`## Agents involved` section, so repeating it in the step is the redundancy the profile removes. Where
the bullet opens with a conditional clause, move the condition to the end or split it into two
sentences. Every rewritten bullet must start with a member of the approved verb list; if the natural
verb is not a member, prefer the closest member over adding a new one, and where a genuinely new verb is
needed, record the addition as a disposition row with the reason and the corpus occurrence that
justifies it rather than adding it silently.

Second, sentence length. Split every procedural sentence over 20 words and every descriptive sentence
over 25 into conforming sentences. Splitting adds a subject and a verb, so it grows bytes; that growth
is expected and is what D-28 requires this phase to measure rather than avoid.

Third, the banned constructions. Replace a modal of obligation in a procedural step with the direct
instruction. Give a bare demonstrative subject an in-sentence antecedent or replace it with the noun.
Replace the and-slash-or construction with whichever it means. Split a chained imperative into one
instruction per sentence.

Do not touch `## Inputs required` or `## Stop conditions` bullets. They are noun phrases and
conditionals by design, they are out of the guard's scope, and reshaping them into imperatives would be
conforming to a rule the profile does not make.

Handle the frozen sections deliberately. `## Stop conditions` and `## Commit` are frozen-set sources: if
a sentence in either genuinely needs the profile, change it, name the companion edit in its disposition
row, and confirm the disposition gate accepts it before moving to the next file. If it does not need the
profile, leave it. Do not bulk-reword either section, and do not skip them on the assumption that they
are exempt — the point of D-02 is that these get the most deliberate treatment, not the least.

Where a `## When to use` first sentence changes, the catalog derives it, so regenerate the catalog in
this plan's commit and let its byte gate confirm.

Write one disposition row per changed clause, citing the WP rule id that drove the change.
