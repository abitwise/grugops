<objective>
Apply the profile to the thirteen hand-authored checklists, and fix four kit files still carrying
vocabulary for a mechanism this project deleted two milestones ago.

The checklists are the smallest part of the governed corpus by bytes — 19,368 across thirteen files —
and the largest by surprise, because the directory also holds a single 89,840-byte generated file that
is 82 percent of it and 345 of the kit's bullets. D-42 excludes that file by a derived marker rather
than by name, and this plan does not open it. Style-rewriting rows copied verbatim from a third-party
standard would falsify the file's own claim about itself, be reverted the next time it is generated, and
triple the denominator so the reported number stopped meaning anything.

D-46 is the other half. Six kit files still carry retired relay vocabulary, and they survived the
earlier vocabulary audit for two structural reasons: that audit covered roles and workflows only, and
the retired-vocabulary authority holds the path form of the deleted directory while these files carry the
prose form. Two of the four this plan fixes are load-bearing — a definition-of-done item requires an
artifact that no longer exists, so a team following the checklist is told to produce something the kit
deleted — and two are prose that narrates a relay the shared verified context replaced. The packaging
document's occurrence is left alone deliberately: it is accurate history, and turning history into a
present-tense claim would be its own defect. That leave-alone is recorded as a named judgement.

Purpose: finish the checklists part of the corpus and close a vocabulary gap two audits missed.
Output: thirteen conforming checklists, four corrected files, one recorded leave-alone, and a
disposition file.
</objective>
