<objective>
Ship the controlled-language guard: two named predicates over a derived 47-file corpus, both watched
failing against the real tree, plus the derived Technical Names set the profile promises.

D-39 settles the name. LANG-04's test is that the guard is named for the decidable subset it checks,
and a name that describes the surface rather than the predicate fails that test in the same shape the
old voice guard's name failed it. So the verb-at-imperative-position predicate is
`guard_imperative_lexicon`, and the sentence-length and banned-construction predicates get their own
name, `guard_sentence_form`. Two predicates, two names, one module.

D-12's framing — adopt the head of a measured distribution and justify the tail — is not available,
because the corpus has no head to adopt: 222 distinct first words over 421 bullets, 176 of them
occurring exactly once, and the nine most frequent are all non-verbs. Four different step grammars
coexist. The structural answer is the same move that finally closed three earlier multi-round
problems in this repository: declare the canonical form and refuse everything outside it. A step
bullet has one legal shape, and conformance is measured against it.

D-36 bounds the corpus and D-42 excludes the generated OWASP checklist by a derived marker. That
exclusion is not a convenience: the file is 89,840 bytes, 82 percent of its directory, and 345 rows
copied verbatim from a third-party standard. Style-rewriting it would be reverted on the next
generation, would falsify its own verbatim-copy claim, and would triple the denominator so the
reported number stopped meaning anything.

D-40 corrects D-13 additively. The listers return filenames, but the Technical Names used in governed
prose are display names, so deriving from the filenames would produce a set that matches nothing in
the corpus. The fix stays inside D-13's spirit — derived, never listed — by deriving the display names
from the heading line each file already carries, and pinning the count two-sided.

D-15 is recorded rather than shipped, in the guard's own source, so a later phase does not add passive
voice as an obvious omission. D-14's modal rule alone would land near-vacuous — seven occurrences in
the entire governed corpus — which is why D-35's two-number sentence-length split is what carries the
RED evidence, at 273 of 982 sentences over the procedural limit.

Purpose: give the corpus-rewrite plans a gate that decides, in one shape, what a conforming step and a
conforming sentence are.
Output: a standalone gate wired at both ends with two named predicates, two new derivation functions,
two two-sided count pins, and two RED transcripts.
</objective>
