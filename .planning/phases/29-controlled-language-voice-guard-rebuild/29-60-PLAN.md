<objective>
Close the round and close the phase, in writing, on the tree.

A verification round reads source and committed artifacts. It does not read a planning conversation. So a
decision that lives only in a conversation — "this residual is accepted", "this remedy was declined and
here is why", "this claim was narrowed rather than the mechanism widened" — is indistinguishable from a
silent drop when the next reader arrives. Rounds 4, 5, 6 and 7 each left a register for exactly that
reason, and this one has a harder job than any of them: it is the last.

Round 8 did four things and refused one. It narrowed the claim `guard_banned_claims` publishes to the
predicate it decides (D-55). It disclosed the hard-wrap axis with derived counts and a direction, and
declined its remedy with a written reason (D-56). It repaired a build-parity gate that could not fail, and
proved the repair on a planted drift that was green before and red after (D-57). And it is the final round:
a finding after this becomes a backlog item or a follow-up phase, not a round 9 (D-58).

**The phase close is the load-bearing half.** Phase 29 ships a set of guards, and the honest question at
the end is not which requirement ids are ticked but which sentences about this kit are true. That statement
belongs in the tree, at the granularity of a mechanism, with every open axis named, counted and directed.

Purpose: this project's value proposition is the trace. A round that does the work and leaves its record
unreconciled has spent the work without banking it.

Output: the disposition table, the roll-up reconciled in both directions, the phase close, the requirement
recommendation that applies nothing, and a sweep whose every number was taken rather than carried.
</objective>
