<objective>
Rewrite the two dual-voice safety roles, flip the three claims Phase 28 deferred here, and take the
role corpus to seventeen of seventeen.

These two files are planned separately for three measured reasons. `security-nfr.md` has the tightest
budget in the phase — 75 bytes to its hard FAIL ceiling today, and the trailing-sentence delete plus
the when-absent sentence alone land it exactly on that ceiling, before the rewrite adds a byte. Both
files carry a caveman fence and clear-voice safety text in one document, and the project's voice
discipline requires the safety half stay clear: the joke never replaces the explanation, and a bleed
here is the one place it would matter most. And both are on the safety-surface exclusion list, so
D-02's rule bites hardest here — safety sections are not exempt from the profile, they receive it
deliberately, one file at a time, each change dispositioned. Freezing them would leave the kit's most
safety-critical prose as its least controlled text, which inverts the requirement at exactly the point
where two agents most need to reach the same act.

The three deferred claims land in the same commits as the text that justifies them, per D-04. D-31
flips the public grug-brained claim once all seventeen blocks carry real voice — the one-job and
hard-limits halves already held; short-words was the half that failed, and this plan is where it stops
failing. D-32 corrects three stale counts from 18 to 17, with the derivation rule carried alongside the
number so a later reader who counts files on disk does not correct it back. D-45 extends the same
correction to the byte figure sitting in the same field, because a row carrying a number no command
reproduces is exactly the defect the count correction exists to fix.

Purpose: finish the role track with the hardest two files and the claims that depend on it.
Output: two rewritten roles, three flipped or corrected registry rows, and the first fully green
aggregator run of the phase.
</objective>
