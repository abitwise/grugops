<objective>
Close F-14 (ledger row 200): a quoted ticket identifier's control byte is DELETED before the reader
sees it, so the published snapshot states twice that a file declares an identifier it does not
declare. A reader who searches the tree for what the sentence prints finds nothing, and a reader who
re-types it produces a different identifier — which is the review's own sentence about a sibling
field, one field over.

The remedy is one builder that escapes by construction (D-07). Every interpolation of a
content-derived value into a published sentence goes through it, across all three board modules, and
the ownership is proved by a DERIVED census rather than by a list somebody maintains.

Two corrections this plan carries, because CONTEXT.md's text does not match the tree. First, D-07
says the site-list derivation is retired: no site-list derivation exists in the source. The "both
sites" claim it refers to is prose in a plan report, and the only derivation-shaped artefact nearby
is the escaper-versus-sanitizer code-point agreement case, which is a two-authority pin and must
SURVIVE. Second, the measured interpolation surface across the three modules is 105 lines mixing
three classes, so a blanket rule over all of them is a different and much larger cutover; this plan
derives the content-derived subset explicitly and names the other two classes as a decision.

Purpose: the difference set between what the ticket grammar refuses and what the renderer deletes is
34 code points, 32 of which are admissible in a ticket value. Every one of those is a snapshot that
misquotes its own evidence.
Output: one exported builder, three rewritten conflict and error sentences, two consumer modules
routed through it, and a derived site census with a stated boundary.
</objective>
