## The derivation (the one stated grep)

A line is a stdout-only allow site when both of these hold:

- It is a non-comment line that matches:
  `/\.not\.toContain\((?:'"deny"'|"deny"|"permissionDecision"|DENY_DECISION)\)|stdout\)\.toBe\(""\)|\bexpectAllow\(/`
- It has no `/\b(?:status|signal|code)\b[^\n]*\.(?:toBe|toBeNull)\(/` assertion on the line itself or in the 10 lines above it.

The shell form of the same predicate is `grep -nE -B10 '<first regex>' FILE`, dropping every hit whose 10-line window carries a status, signal or code assertion.

Counts before the plan (commit `7bf17690`):

| File | Lines found | Made up of |
|------|-------------|------------|
| `hooks/guard.test.ts` | 46 | 1 helper assertion, 25 helper calls, 5 `not.toContain("deny")`, 8 `stdout).toBe("")`, 4 `replay`, 3 `runAll` consumers |
| `hooks/admission-guard.test.ts` | 16 | 1 helper assertion, 15 helper calls |

After the plan, the count is **0** in each file, and a test asserts it.

The already-safe sites were left alone. Each of these carries a status assertion beside it: guard.test.ts pre-plan lines 328-331, 472, 1312 and 1534, and the notify-tier line after `expect(r.status).toBe(0)`.

The research doc's figure of 22 counts each helper once and not its call sites. With the call sites counted, the same predicate gives 46 + 16.
