## How these rows were made

The gate was run as `node scripts/check-diff-disposition.js` with no row in this file, and its output was read from a file. It gave
23 findings, and each has one row. The gate compares whole lines, so the 13 sentences of Workflow 04 step 16 that stayed word for
word owe a row each: their `before` and `after` are the same text. Step 16 sat on line 50 at the base and sits on line 55 now. A
sentence plan 41-04 or plan 41-11 added and this plan rewrites leaves that plan's row stale, and its replacement has its row here.
The Orchestrator and role-switch lines drew no finding, because the role-rule stand-in admitted them; each has a row because this
file records every clause the plan changed.

Every row here is about the queue, a stop or a refusal, so each reads `pending (named human):` followed by the agent's comparison.
That comparison is advice. The disposition is the named human's, and it stays pending until the named human reads the row against
the diff. No changed clause sits in a frozen section: `## Stop conditions` and `## Commit` of Workflows 04 and 17 and `## Hard
limits` of the Orchestrator did not change. So every `companion` cell is `—`.
