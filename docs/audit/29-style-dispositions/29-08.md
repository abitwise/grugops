# Plan 29-08 style dispositions

The first six workflows — `00-bootstrap-greenfield.md`, `01-bootstrap-brownfield.md`,
`02-idea-to-epics.md`, `03-epic-to-tickets.md`, `04-ticket-to-pr.md` and `05-pr-quality-gate.md` —
put on the writing profile: every `## Steps` bullet on the canonical step form (WP-01), every
procedural sentence at or under 20 words and every descriptive sentence at or under 25 (WP-02,
WP-03, WP-04), and the banned constructions removed (WP-05, WP-06, WP-08).

**The judgement is written here; the table below is only its coverage.** A row proves that a changed
clause was considered. It proves nothing about whether the consideration was right — that is a named
human reading these rows against the diff, which is what LANG-03's manual verification is.

## What the conversion actually is

Four transformations account for every row.

**1. The canonical step form.** A `## Steps` bullet begins with a verb from the closed approved set,
in bare imperative form, at position zero. The bullets in these six files opened four other ways: a
bold label (`**Run the gate** in order: …`), a determiner (`The Orchestrator pulls the ticket …`), an
actor subject (`BA/PM defines the product …`) and a leading conditional (`When the behavior is
unclear, …`). The label comes off and the verb it decorated moves to position zero. The determiner
and the actor subject are re-narrated as instructions. The conditional moves behind its instruction.

**The actor is not deleted; it moves.** Where a workflow runs several agents in sequence, knowing
*which* agent performs a step is load-bearing, so the actor is kept as a trailing parenthetical —
`Draft \`memory-bank/greenfield-plan.md\` from the raw idea (Greenfield Mapper)`. That is the shape
`guard_imperative_lexicon`'s own remedy text prescribes: *"name the actor later in the sentence if it
is load-bearing."* In `02-idea-to-epics.md`, where `## Agents involved` names exactly one agent, the
parenthetical is redundant and was dropped instead.

**2. Sentence splitting.** A step over 20 words, or a paragraph sentence over 25, is split into
conforming sentences. Splitting adds a subject and a verb, so it grows bytes. That growth is measured
per file in the plan summary rather than avoided, which is what D-28 requires of this phase.

**3. Modal removal (WP-05).** Five modals sat inside procedural steps in this batch, all in
`05-pr-quality-gate.md`: `must pass`, `can still reach`, `may not do it`, `could not run`, `may not
self-author`. **Three of the five were prohibitions**, and each is now stated with `never` rather
than with a modal — *"the agent never does it"*, *"the agent never self-authors a justification to
clear its own gate"*. That is stronger than the modal it replaces, not weaker, which is the direction
a safety rule is allowed to move under a style pass. The other two were a permission (`can still
reach` → *"still reaches"*) and a description of exit code `2` (`could not run` → *"failed to run"*).

**4. A machine-visible sentence boundary.** The sentence splitter ends a sentence at `.`, `!` or `?`
followed by whitespace. Two constructions in these files defeat it and produced merged, over-long
units that no amount of rewording inside them could fix:

- a quoted request ending `… seed the board."` — the character before the space is a quotation mark,
  so the next sentence merges into it;
- a bold label ending `**Deterministic prefetch.**` — the character before the space is an asterisk.

Neither was solved by moving a period outside its quotation mark, and neither was solved by hiding
the text in a fence. In `00` and `01` the `## When to use` paragraph was **reordered** so the quoted
Orchestrator request ends it; in `05` three bold labels that survive as emphasis (`**Verdict
emission (green result only)**`, `**No faked pass (escape hatch)**`, `**Advisory composition**`,
`**Updating a visual baseline is human-only**`) carry their full stop **outside** the bold rather
than inside it. The words are unchanged in every case.

## What was deliberately NOT touched

- **`## Inputs required` bullets.** They are noun phrases by design and out of
  `guard_imperative_lexicon`'s scope. Rewriting them into imperatives would be conforming to a rule
  the profile does not make. Bullet counts unchanged in all six files: 3 / 3 / 3 / 3 / 3 / 5.
- **`## Stop conditions` bullets are NOT rewritten into imperatives.** They are conditionals by
  design and the check is section-scoped. Two of them were touched at all, and only for sentence
  length — see below. Bullet counts unchanged in all six files: 1 / 2 / 1 / 2 / 2 / 3.
- **Passive voice.** Deliberately not banned (D-15). The kit's own correct prose is saturated with
  it, and a passive rewrite would change large volumes of accurate text for no decidable gain.
- **The generated OWASP checklist.** Not in this plan's files and not in the governed corpus.
- **Every guard predicate and every scan set.** `git diff` touches no file under `scripts/`. The
  governed corpus is still 47 files in four parts.

## The frozen sections, one judgement at a time

`## Stop conditions` and `## Commit` are frozen positionally by the disposition contract, in all
nineteen workflows. D-02 is why they are touched at all rather than exempted: leaving the kit's most
load-bearing prose as its least controlled text inverts LANG-02 at exactly the point where two agents
most need to reach the same act. **45 rows below carry a `companion` cell.** They are two kinds and
nothing else.

**`## Stop conditions` — two bullets, in `00` and `02`, both the same shape.** Each was a single
27-word sentence joining an act and a prohibition with a semicolon:

> `- The idea is too vague to map — stop and request the missing clarity from the requester; do not invent the user, the pain, or the value.`

The semicolon became a full stop. **The withheld permission is byte-identical** — `Do not invent the
user, the pain, or the value.` — and the bullet is still a conditional, not an imperative. Nothing
else in either section changed, and neither section's bullet count moved.

**`## Commit` — all six files, the same shape.** Each opened with a 38-to-65-word instruction that
carried its artifact enumeration in a parenthetical. The enumeration became its own sentence. In
every one of the six, **the branch guard and `Never merge, never deploy; humans hold both.` are
byte-unchanged**; in `04-ticket-to-pr.md` the branch-guard parenthetical became its own sentence
(`Never a protected branch: the implementation already lives on a \`grugops/ticket-to-pr-<id>\`
working branch per \`autonomy=pr\`.`) because the original ran to 52 words, and its guard, branch
name and `autonomy=pr` rule all survive intact.

**No safety rule was compressed to make a sentence bound.** Where a rule would not survive a split,
the split went somewhere else in the sentence. Where a prohibition's modal had to go, the
prohibition got `never`.

## The one clause that was dropped rather than dispositioned

`02-idea-to-epics.md` step 2 ended `…; says no to bloat`. Splitting the sentence would have promoted
that to a standalone `Say no to bloat.`, which normalizes to the exact clause
`agent-factory/roles/ba-pm.md` states in its own `## Hard limits`. The gate reported it as a frozen
intersection the moment it was written — a **workflow copy of a role's prohibition**, minted by a
style pass. Writing a companion cell for it was available and was refused: WP-10 and D-19 give a
prohibition exactly one home, and the register would then have recorded the duplication as
considered rather than as prevented. The clause was dropped from the step. The prohibition stays in
`ba-pm.md`, byte-unchanged, as its sole home.

## Two recording conventions, stated rather than left to be noticed

**The pipe character.** This register is itself a markdown table, and
`readDispositionRows()` splits a row on `|` — so a cell containing a literal pipe silently produces
the wrong cell count and the row is not read at all. Two rows quote
`` `off | ui-or-critical-path | always` ``; both write `/` in place of `|`. `normalizeSentence()`
folds every non-alphanumeric character to a space, so `|` and `/` normalize identically and the match
is unaffected. This is recorded here because a silently-unread row is exactly the failure the
seven-column count exists to catch.

**Why a row exists for text that did not change.** A workflow paragraph is a **single source line**.
`git diff --unified=0` reports that whole line as touched when any one clause on it moves, and the
gate then derives every clause on it. So an unchanged sentence sitting on the same line as a changed
one gets a row, marked as unchanged. That is the same accounting plan 29-07 recorded, at six times
the volume, because these paragraphs are long.
