## Why this file changed

`31-16` closes `CR-09` (both variants), `CR-10` and `IN-10` from `31-REVIEW.md`, each independently
reproduced in `31-VERIFICATION.md` round 4 against the committed `.js`. The recipe's prose was a live
defect in three places, and one of them asserted the OPPOSITE of what was true:

- **The call-link paragraph** stated the marker's routing property as though it held for the rule,
  when it held for the head-and-tail arm only. The two whole-path arms compared the marker-carrying
  path as a literal, so one legitimate `.configure()` link walked past both.
- **The "Refused in a `*.uat.spec.ts` file" list** named the call-link and rename routes to the
  modifier family but not the TestInfo FIXTURE PARAMETER, which is the framework's primary documented
  spelling of the same modifiers. A reader was told the family was decided; two thirds of the ways to
  reach it were.
- **The completeness bullet on the reverse walk's boundary** said a call-link spelling was outside
  the denominator "even though the rule refuses that spelling". For the two whole-path arms the rule
  did not refuse it. That sentence is the claim-broader-than-the-mechanism defect this requirement
  exists to prevent, made inside the paragraph whose job is to disclose a boundary.

The "Deliberately outside the rule" list is quoted verbatim from `UNRESOLVABLE_CALLEE_RESIDUALS` and
moves with it: one member's text was extended (the parser-predicate degradation now also covers the
function-like predicates the fixture-parameter derivation reads) and two members were added (a
destructured second parameter, and the scope-unawareness of both canonicalisations). The
both-directions equality case in `scripts/runnable-ref/uat-spec-integrity.test.ts` keeps the two in
step; it was not narrowed.

Security-relevant explanatory text in this checklist is written in CLEAR voice, not caveman voice,
per the project's voice discipline: the joke is reserved for text that is not a safety explanation.
